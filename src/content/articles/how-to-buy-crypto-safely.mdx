The app is open. The button is green. A number is sitting in the box.

You do not know the company's legal name. You do not know the fee. You do not know who can spend the crypto five minutes from now.

Close the app for a minute. This page is that minute.

The [CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnAndProtect/AdvisoriesAndArticles/understand_risks_of_virtual_currency.html) tells people not to buy products they do not understand. It also says to speculate only with money they can afford to lose. If someone says there is no chance of loss, the same advisory says not to invest.

## Start with the money, not the logo

Write down a number you could lose without missing rent, food, a debt payment, or a doctor.

That number is the ceiling. Not a goal.

Crypto is not a bank balance. The [FDIC](https://www.fdic.gov/resources/deposit-insurance/financial-products-not-insured) lists crypto assets among products it does not insure, even if you bought them from an FDIC-insured bank. The price can fall. Deposit insurance does not fill that hole.

If the only money available is this month's rent, you are done with this page. That is a complete outcome.

## Name the asset before you name the app

[Investor.gov](https://www.investor.gov/additional-resources/spotlight/crypto-assets) says crypto assets are not all alike. A coin, a token, and a fund that holds coins are different things. Different risks.

If the asset is bitcoin, read [What is Bitcoin?](/articles/what-is-bitcoin/) first. If someone cannot explain the asset without a promise, you do not understand it yet. The CFTC's test still applies.

## Look the company up before you make the login

A polished app is not a background check.

The [CFTC's red-flag guide](https://www.cftc.gov/sites/default/files/LearnandProtect/DigitalAssetRedFlags.pdf) says digital-asset trading platforms must register as money services businesses with FinCEN, and often with states. Search [FinCEN's MSB registrant search](https://msb.fincen.gov/). Read the notice on that page. Inclusion is not a recommendation, a certification, or an endorsement.

Registration is not a force field. The [CFTC's fraud page](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnAndProtect/digitalassetfrauds) says relying on registration alone will not protect you, and that most scams involve people and products that are not registered.

If a person is selling you advice, not just operating a checkout screen, look them up too.

- State securities regulator, via [NASAA's contact list](https://www.nasaa.org/contact-your-regulator/).
- [AdviserInfo](https://adviserinfo.sec.gov/), the SEC's adviser database.
- [BrokerCheck](https://brokercheck.finra.org/), from FINRA.
- [NFA BASIC](https://www.nfa.futures.org/basicnet/), for futures firms.

No record does not prove a scam. A record of problems is a reason to stop. The red-flag guide says most scams involve unregistered people. Take the absence seriously, then keep reading.

## Type the address yourself

Do not tap the link in the text. Do not scan the square code in the email.

The [CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnAndProtect/digitalassetfrauds) says those can be phishing: a look-alike site built to take the login or the money. Search for the company, or type the address you already confirmed.

If a site asks for a photo of your ID, that can be normal at a regulated firm, and it can also be a thief. Confirm the site before you upload anything. The CFTC tells people to research a platform before they hand over sensitive personal information.

## Turn the lock on before you deposit

Passwords get guessed. A second check helps.

The [SEC custody bulletin](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins/crypto-asset-custody-basics-retail-investors-investor-bulletin-0) tells retail investors to use strong passwords and multi-factor authentication. Prefer an authenticator app over a text message when the platform offers both.

Then the rule that sounds obvious until someone is on the phone.

Do not share the private key. Do not share the seed phrase. Not with "support." Not with a new friend. The SEC says a lost private key is permanent loss of access. A shared one is worse. The other person can move the funds.

The [glossary entry for seed phrase](/glossary/#seed-phrase) is three sentences. Read it before a wallet shows you a list of secret words.

## Read the number on the confirm screen

Headline prices skip the spread and the fee.

The [CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnAndProtect/digitalassetfrauds) says to understand what you are being sold, including the risk of loss, the costs, and the fees. Read the platform's own fee schedule. Look at the total on the confirm screen before you accept it.

If you cannot find the fee, you are not ready to tap the button.

## Decide who keeps the secret

This is the part apps rush past.

The [SEC bulletin](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins/crypto-asset-custody-basics-retail-investors-investor-bulletin-0) describes three shapes.

A hot wallet is online. Convenient. More exposed to hacks.

A cold wallet is offline. Less convenient. Generally safer from online attacks. You can still lose the device.

A company can hold the keys. If that company is hacked, shuts down, or goes bankrupt, you may lose access.

This page does not pick one. Beginners lose money both by leaving coins with a firm that fails and by mismanaging a seed phrase on day one. Read both failures before you choose.

## Write down what a tax return may need

The purchase can matter at tax time even if you never sell.

The [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/filing/digital-assets) says digital assets are property, not currency, and that you should keep records of purchases, sales, and the dollar value. Brokers report some proceeds on [Form 1099-DA](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1099-da) for transactions on or after January 1, 2025.

Save four things the day you buy: the date, the asset, the amount, and the dollar value including fees.

That is record-keeping, not tax advice. A fuller orientation is on [Taxes and rules](/pillars/taxes-and-rules/).

## If a person is steering your thumb, stop

You are allowed to close the laptop.

The CFTC's 2026 note, [Pause Before You Pay](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnAndProtect/AdvisoriesAndArticles/ATM.html), is about odd payment instructions. Hang up. Contact the company with a number you look up yourself. Do not use the number in the message.

Be especially wary of a wallet address you did not create. The advisory says a transfer to an address cannot be reversed.

The patterns are spelled out in [Crypto scams to avoid](/articles/crypto-scams-to-avoid/).

## A slow first purchase, in order

If you are still going ahead, go in this order. Skip a step and you are guessing.

1. Write the most you can lose. The [CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnAndProtect/AdvisoriesAndArticles/understand_risks_of_virtual_currency.html) says that is the only money to speculate with.
2. Name the asset. Read a primary explanation, not a thread.
3. Look the company up on the registers above.
4. Type the website yourself.
5. Turn on multi-factor sign-in before you deposit.
6. Read the fee on the confirm screen.
7. Decide, for this amount only, who holds the key.
8. Save the records the same day.
9. Stop if a stranger is telling you which buttons to press.

A small first amount is a way to learn the screens. It is not a strategy, and that amount can still be lost.
